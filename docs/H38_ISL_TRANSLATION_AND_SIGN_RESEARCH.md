# H38 — Indian Sign Language (ISL) Translation, Cross-Language Discovery & Sign Research

**Milestone:** H38  
**Focus:** Grounded Linguistic Research, Cross-Language Sign Discovery, Clause Structure & Motion Architecture  

---

## 1. Research Objectives

1. **Clause Boundary & Subordinate Clause Syntax in ISL:**
   - How does ISL structure complex sentences containing conditionals ("if..."), purpose clauses ("to help you..."), and relative clauses ("what you are about to watch...")?
   - How does negation interact with complex sentences? (Negation scope must be bound to the negated clause, not exported to the sentence boundary).
2. **Relative Clauses vs Interrogative WH-Questions:**
   - In spoken English, "what" serves as both an interrogative pronoun ("What is your name?") and a relative/fused relative pronoun ("What you're about to watch is...").
   - In ISL, interrogative WH-signs (`WHAT`, `HOW`, `WHERE`) are placed clause-finally with non-manual markers (furrowed brows, head tilt). Relative clauses use topic-marking or pointing/relative indexes (`THAT`, `WHICH`, `TOPIC`), NEVER sentence-final interrogative `WHAT`.
3. **Cross-Language Sign Discovery Methodology:**
   - When an ISL sign is absent from existing dictionaries, research how the concept is expressed across sign languages (ISL corpora, ASL, BSL, Auslan, International Sign).
   - Use cross-language examples as semantic and morphological clues (handshape iconicity, movement plane, spatial referencing) while cross-referencing with ISL linguistic tendencies.
   - Author authentic Schema 2.0.0 signs with transparent provenance.
4. **Number and Counting Systems in ISL:**
   - ISL uses base-10 counting with distinctive hand configurations:
     - 1–10: Single-hand configurations in the signing space.
     - 20, 30, 40...: Two-digit compounds (e.g., `2` + `0` or decade base signs) or fluid double-digit flicking.
     - 100 (`HUNDRED`), 1,000 (`THOUSAND`): Distinct base signs combined with multiplicand numerals.
5. **Discourse Markers vs Content Verbs:**
   - Conversational markers like "You see...", "Well...", "Look..." serve pragmatic discourse functions (establishing shared attention/context) rather than physical perception actions. They must be mapped to attention-getting markers (`LOOK`, `NOTICE`, `TOPIC-MARKER`) or absorbed into discourse prosody.

## 3. Linguistic Rules for Complex Sentence Restructuring

### Rule 1: Clause-Scoped Negation (`ISL-CLAUSE-NEG-01`)
- **Principle:** In a compound or complex sentence consisting of subordinate clause $C_{\text{sub}}$ and main clause $C_{\text{main}}$, a negative particle occurring inside $C_{\text{sub}}$ must be placed at the boundary of $C_{\text{sub}}$, NOT moved to the end of $C_{\text{main}}$.
- **Application on Segment 10:**
  - English: "Hey, if we haven't met before, my name is David JP Phillips,"
  - $C_{\text{sub}}$: "if we haven't met before" $\to$ `BEFORE WE MEET NOT`
  - $C_{\text{main}}$: "my name is David JP Phillips" $\to$ `MY NAME [DAVID JP PHILLIPS]`
  - Correct ISL Sequence: `BEFORE WE MEET NOT, MY NAME [DAVID] [JP] [PHILLIPS]`
  - Contrast with H37 Defect: `BEFORE HEY WE HAVE MEET MY NAME NOT` (which meant "My name is NOT David").

### Rule 2: Relative Clause vs Interrogative Disambiguation (`ISL-REL-CLAUSE-01`)
- **Principle:** When "what" introduces a free relative clause (e.g., "What you're about to watch is..."), it is functioning as the syntactic subject/object of the main clause ("The content you are about to watch"). It must NOT be treated as a WH-question word.
- **Application on Segment 04:**
  - English: "What you're about to watch is a special compilation..."
  - Meaning: "You watch soon, special compilation..."
  - Correct ISL Sequence: `YOU WATCH SOON, SPECIAL COMPILATION THREE DAY PUBLIC-SPEAKING CHALLENGE`
  - Contrast with H37 Defect: Ending with interrogative `WATCH WHAT` with furrowed brows.

### Rule 3: Discourse Marker Absorption (`ISL-DISCOURSE-MARKER-01`)
- **Principle:** Spoken English idioms and conversational starters such as "You see,...", "Well,...", "Look,..." must not be parsed as literal transitive actions.
- **Application on Segment 14:**
  - English: "You see, I've studied over 5,000 speakers..."
  - "You see" is a conversational attention cue, not visual perception. It should be translated as `NOTICE` or absorbed into the Time-Topic frame.
  - Correct ISL Sequence: `I STUDY OVER FIVE THOUSAND SPEAKER, PINPOINT GREAT SPEAKER HOW`
  - Contrast with H37 Defect: Ending with `... GREAT SPEAKER SEE`.

### Rule 4: Compound Number Decomposition (`ISL-NUM-DECOMP-01`)
- **Principle:** Multi-digit numbers must be decomposed into their constituent linguistic numeral signs rather than truncated:
  - `110` $\to$ `HUNDRED` `TEN` (or `ONE` `ONE` `ZERO`)
  - `30` $\to$ `THIRTY` (or `THREE` `ZERO`)
  - `25` $\to$ `TWENTY` `FIVE` (or `TWO` `FIVE`)
  - `5,000` $\to$ `FIVE` `THOUSAND`
